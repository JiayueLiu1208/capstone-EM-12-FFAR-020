Four servos, one pen, a lot of calibration.

A desktop robot arm that writes. Type a word and it plans every stroke, solves its own joint angles, and puts the word on paper with a fineliner.

The arm is a four-axis articulated manipulator. The base turns on a thrust bearing, the shoulder and elbow pitch the two printed links, and a small wrist servo counter-rotates so the pen always points straight down. Between strokes the whole arm lifts the pen 12 mm and travels without leaving a mark.

Everything runs from a laptop: the planner turns text into strokes and joint angles, and an Arduino receives the angles over USB and drives the four servos through a PCA9685 PWM board.

The hard part was accuracy. Cheap servos have dead band and backlash, so most of the build went into calibration, compensation, and slowing down where the arm changes direction.

02 · Program sequence

From keystroke to ink in five stations.

  1. Input

    A word typed into the web interface or passed on the command line.

  2. Glyphs

    Each letter becomes single-line strokes from a font with a 10-unit cap height.

  3. Plan

    Strokes are ordered and flipped to cut pen-up travel, then resampled every 0.5 mm.

  4. Solve

    Inverse kinematics turns every sample (x, y, z) into four joint angles, θ₁ to θ₄.

  5. Drive

    Angles become PWM pulse widths on four channels at 50 Hz. The pen lifts 12 mm in Z between strokes.

03 · Kinematics

Five lines of maths run the whole arm.

J1 base0.0°
J2 shoulder0.0°
J3 elbow0.0°
J4 wrist0.0°

The base angle comes straight from the bearing of the pen target. That leaves a two-link problem in the vertical plane through the pen. Both links are L = 150 mm, so shoulder, elbow and wrist form an isosceles triangle and one arccos solves it. The pen hangs 96 mm below the wrist, the same as the shoulder height, so when it writes the wrist is level with the shoulder and the elbow sits halfway to the pen.

θ₁ = atan2(x, y)
r = √(x² + y²), h = z + Lₜ − h₀ = z
β = arccos(√(r² + h²) / 2L)
θ₂ = atan2(h, r) + β, θ₃ = −2β
θ₄ = −90° − θ₂ − θ₃

The arm in the work cell above solves the same equations every frame, with a light wand in place of the pen. The figures on the left are its live joint angles.
